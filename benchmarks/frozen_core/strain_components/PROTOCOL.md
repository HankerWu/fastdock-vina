# Paired electrostatic decomposition of intraligand strain

Post-hoc diagnosis of the already-published discrepancy between BACE1 and Factor Xa. Frozen before running the decomposition. No fitted coefficients and no default-score change.

Use both BACE1 Ki assays from the fresh native first-pose archive and all included Factor Xa Ki compounds from CHEMBL661804, both receptors and the two original seeds. Inputs, ranks, stereochemistry, microstates and free-conformer search (MMFF94s, eight ETKDGv3 conformers, seed 777) are fixed to the archived procedure. Hydrogen positions relax with full MMFF at fixed heavy coordinates. Select the gas-conformer reference using the full potential. At each of those identical bound and reference geometries, evaluate the MMFF electrostatic term by subtracting the energy with MMFF electrostatics disabled from the full energy. Do not relax or reselect a conformer after deleting the component.

The test asks whether the differential vacuum intraligand electrostatic term accounts for the transfer discrepancy, particularly across the two assigned formal-charge classes in Factor Xa. For every native first pose compare (a) native; (b) full unit replacement; (c) the same replacement less the normalized differential electrostatic term. A fourth complementary diagnostic replaces the native internal difference by the electrostatic component alone. These ablations assign numerical dependence of the frozen scores and do not estimate the solution dielectric or physical solvation free energy.

Reproduce the historical native/full pairwise totals before interpreting the ablations. Report all individual scores, differences between independent reconstruction and archived full/reference energies, receptor-specific Spearman and Kendall tau-b coefficients, all >=3-fold assay-pair signs, same-charge and equal-charge/size subsets, and grouped leave-one-ligand-out sensitivity where supported. Use median across the same seed pairs; preserve the historical Factor Xa joint endpoint as median of the four within-seed/receptor differences. Never treat pair signs or receptor repeats as independent samples.
